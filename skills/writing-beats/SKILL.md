# Writing Beats

Develop a piece through small, sequential moves instead of drafting the whole article in one pass.

1. Read the available raw material and current draft before proposing a beat.
2. Establish what the target reader can reasonably be assumed to know. Track concepts introduced by the draft so later beats do not depend on ideas the reader has not encountered.
3. Offer 2 or 3 distinct candidate starting beats when no opening is fixed. Explain what each beat accomplishes and what it enables next.
4. After the user selects a beat, write only that beat to the draft.
5. Reread the draft, then offer 2 or 3 reachable next beats. A beat is reachable when the reader has enough context for it and it advances the piece rather than repeating prior work.
6. Keep each beat to one meaningful move: scene, claim, example, question, contrast, explanation, objection, or turn. Split a beat that is doing several unrelated jobs.
7. Preserve user edits between turns. Allow rewrites or branching from an earlier beat without silently rewriting unaffected sections.
8. End when the piece has delivered its central claim or narrative payoff, not when every raw note has been consumed.

Completion criterion: the draft advances through coherent small moves, every beat is understandable from what came before, and the user retains control over the direction at each branch.
